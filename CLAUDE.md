# About possession-store

## What it owns

An inventory of the things the operator owns, on `127.0.0.1:8341` with no auth: dash is the front door. `CONTRACT.md` is the route table and the source of truth for every shape.

It owns possessions and their kinds (twenty: garments, computers, computer parts, audio devices, musical instruments, vehicles, furniture, fitness equipment, sports gear, tabletop games, media, collectibles, art and decor, home textiles, appliances, personal care, bags and cases, electronic accessories, household tools, music accessories — a table each, with its own id prefix; `KindTypes` in `records.go` is the list), outfits, products (a make and model with its listing), purchases and their lines (not eBay orders, which are budget-store's), and the photo review (reviews, model readings, sightings, owner questions). It does not own photos (photo-store), places (location-store), machines (harness-store), what kind of instrument something is (music-practice-store), listing pages (article-store) or orders and the charges that paid for them (budget-store): it stores their ids and checks each one against the owning store before writing it. Net worth is not here yet; it waits on budget-store.

A new kind is a new table and a new entry in `KindTypes` (`records.go`), never a column holding the kind's name. Add one when enough possessions share facts worth searching on; until then a possession with no kind is complete. The fourteen after vehicles came on 2026-11-06, when the first 52 photos left 223 of 288 possessions with no kind; `POST /possessions/classify-kindless` sorted the ones already recorded. A room (`Rooms`) is on the possession itself, never on a kind.

# How it works

## Photo review

Two model-store roles read each queued photo: `possession-photo-reader` (claude-opus-5-5) and `possession-photo-second-reader` (gpt-6.2-astra), chosen by the operator on 2026-11-06 so that two makers' models must agree; the operator moved the second reader from gpt-6-astra to gpt-6.2-astra on 2026-11-17. Each holds one model and no fallback. Calls go through llm-bridge-server's `POST /oneshot` with the service token from the unit's host-local drop-in; costs come from model-store list prices.

Round one: each reader lists what it sees on its own. Round two: each sees both lists and the inventory and writes a final list. `recordReconciliation` (`review.go`) pairs the two lists. What both agree on is recorded at once; a doubt either raised about it becomes an optional **detail** question, whose answer in words amends the possession. The rest waits on a **decision** question whose choices carry their resolution. An answer in words goes to the first reader, which turns it into a resolution. The operator chose on 2026-11-06 that agreement records without waiting: holding agreed things for a detail made 62% of sightings into questions. Settling a sighting settles every later one waiting on it (`same_as_other_sighting`).

The vocabularies in `vocabulary.go` feed both validation and the readers' prompt (`kindGuide`): change them there and nowhere else. A reader's field that does not fit is dropped and named in `dropped_fields`, never stored.

**Outfit reading** (`reading: outfit`, `outfit_reading.go`): one reader, the first role, reads a photo of the wearer (`wearer_person_id`, located by photo-store's confirmed face) against every wearable already recorded. The operator chose one reader for outfits on 2026-11-06; it costs about $0.09 a selfie. New pieces start `unconfirmed` with a "do you still have it?" detail question, because a photo shows the wearer had a thing then, not now. Since 2026-11-07 each piece the reader lists is then matched by picture: both roles see the piece cut from the photo beside the covers of up to 12 recorded wearables of its kind and sub-type (`matchPieces`); both pick the same one, it is linked; both say none, it is new; they differ, a decision question (`asked_by: picture_match`). That adds roughly $0.10 a piece compared (an estimate from token counts, not measured). A reading's pieces find the live outfit with exactly the same pieces, else the same day's outfit sharing the most pieces (at least one), else make a new one, and add a wearing on the photo's day in `POSSESSION_STORE_OWNER_TIME_ZONE`. Wearable kinds (`WearableKinds`): garment, jewelry, watch, eyewear, bag_or_case; an outfit holds nothing else.

## Wearable audit

`wearable_audit.go` and `outfit_consolidation.go` hold three passes over what outfit readings recorded, asked for on 2026-11-07 after one reader matching by text left 65 outfits for 133 selfies, several per day, and likely duplicate pieces: `POST /wearables/check-links`, `POST /wearables/check-duplicates` and `POST /outfits/consolidate`. Each shows pictures to both reader roles. The operator chose: where both agree, act at once, and every merge must be undoable; where they differ, ask a decision question. So a merge of possessions writes a `possession_merge` and a combine of outfits an `outfit_merge`, each naming every row it moved (`merge.go`, `outfit_merge.go`), and `POST /possession-merges/{id}/undo` and `POST /outfit-merges/{id}/undo` put them back, newest first. A question the audit asks carries `asked_by`, and its choices carry resolutions of their own (`auditActions`: `keep_link`, `merge_possessions`, `keep_apart`, `combine_outfits`, `keep_outfits_apart`, and `unlink_possession_id` on accept, same_as or reject); `TriageOutfitDoubts` leaves those alone. A pair or link asked about once is not asked again. CONTRACT.md has the rules for agreement and the batching of large groups.

Agents run the passes on request; nothing schedules them. They answer what they did and `cost_usd`, and run until done, so call them with a long timeout.

**Closed groups and the rematch** (`wearable_groups.go`, `picture_match.go`), asked for on 2026-11-07 after the passes: selfies had left 265 outfit pieces `proposed` behind open decision questions, so they never joined their outfits, and wrist crops matched against close-up covers had made 8 `unconfirmed` watches for an owner of 5. The owner can close a group (`PUT /wearable-groups/{group}`, e.g. `watch`, `jewelry:ring`): then nothing in it is recorded as new anywhere — the picture match shows both readers every owned member with up to 6 photos each and they pick one or say `cannot_tell`, accepting a new piece is a 409, and the link and duplicate checks offer only members. `POST /outfit-pieces/rematch` re-runs the two-reader match on every stuck outfit piece, settling and answering what both agree on (`answered_by: picture_match`), and folds each unconfirmed member of a closed group into the owned member both readers pick, asking otherwise (`asked_by: closed_group`).

## Looks, slots and identification

`looks.go`, `identify.go`, `slot_links.go` and `rebuild.go` replaced the rematch on 2026-11-07, after the operator asked for every outfit to be filled with a best guess before they validate anything. How many pieces the owner has in each group is data: `expected_low`/`expected_high` on `PUT /wearable-groups/{group}`, never in a prompt. Groups follow how the owner counts (`wearableGroup`): pendants and chains are necklaces, scarves are their own group, socks are in none.

Run in order: set the groups' ranges; `POST /outfit-looks/read` (both readers read each day's selfies together into looks, one per set of clothes, and slots, one per piece worn, with a box per photo; the first lists, the second checks and writes the final list); `POST /outfit-slots/identify` (per group, both readers sort every slot crop and recorded cover into individual pieces; the first reader's sorting is the best guess, the second's and both readers' hints rank the rest; each slot is linked to its top candidate with `decided_by: best_guess` and gets one optional question, `asked_by: outfit_slots`, whose answer relinks it); `POST /outfits/rebuild-from-looks` (every look placed again into outfits by their main pieces, undoable through `POST /outfit-rebuilds/{id}/undo`). CONTRACT.md has the scoring, the batch sizes and the rules for merging recorded pieces.

Three faults the first live run showed on 2026-11-07, and what changed. (1) Shirts came out as 83 pieces from 73 slots: when no batch found two pictures alike, the batches were never sorted together. Now one picture of every piece, singletons included, is always sorted across batches, in pairs of chunks when they do not fit one call (`sortEveryPairOfChunks`), so two crops that never shared a batch can still be one piece. (2) Necklaces came out as 26 for an owner of 1–9. Now a group left above its expected high gets one more round (`pullGroupIntoRange`): both readers see one picture per piece, are told the range and that the sorting split too finely, and only what both put together is merged, undoably, owned kept over unconfirmed. It runs on groups with no new slots too, so identification run again refines what is there. A possession merge now repoints the slots linked to the duplicate. (3) 72 outfits, one worn twice: rebuild made one outfit per identical set of pieces, and accessories and what is in frame vary by photo. Now an outfit is known by its top, bottom, one-piece and outerwear (`outfitIdentityGroups`); a look joins one when the main pieces both show agree and at least two match (one is enough for a look showing only one main piece, on a day the outfit was already worn), and the outfit holds the union of its looks' pieces. Slot answers follow the same rule (`placeLook`).

Not an outfit: the owner said on 2026-11-07 never to take them with no top on as an outfit, since those are body check photos. The look readers say per look `wearer_has_no_top`; a look with that, or with no top, one-piece or outerwear slot, is kept but marked `not_an_outfit` by the rebuild, taken out of any outfit, and an outfit left with no look and no reading is removed (`whyNotAnOutfit`, `rebuild.go`). Undo puts it all back.

Second round, same day, after the operator found 67 outfits each worn on one day: two outfits with the same eight pieces stayed apart, since a rebuild placed only looks with no outfit; and one plaid shirt two days apart did not join, since the trousers were out of view in one. Now every rebuild re-places every look, keeping the outfit most of a group's looks were in and snapshotting each outfit it changes for undo (`outfitSnapshot`); one shared top, one-piece or outerwear makes an outfit across days unless a main piece seen in both differs (`identityMatch`); a look of screenshots only (`WhyACopy`: photo-store file name or tag) adds no day; favorite, tags and the owner's cover pick come along (`carryOwnerMarks`). A slot is linked only to a piece a reader put it with: in a closed group every member used to be a choice at one score and the first by id was linked (`no_pick`, `unlinkUnpickedBestGuesses`). `reading: look` queues any photo of the owner — others' photos and group photos too — for the look reader; the review worker leaves it, and a day is read 12 photos at a time.

## Outfit covers

`outfit_covers.go`, asked for on 2026-11-07 so dash's outfits page can show each outfit as one tall photo. Every outfit with a photo answers `cover_photo_id`, `cover_box` and `cover_reason`, worked out on each read from boxes already stored, with no model call: of its photos, the one where the most of its pieces have a box in `possession_photos` (slot links and readings both write there), then the one those boxes span most of, then the earliest. The owner's pick (`PUT /outfits/{id}/cover`, `outfit_cover_choices`) wins while that photo is still one of the outfit's; a rebuild or combine makes new outfit ids, which start without a pick. `GET /outfits/{id}/image` cuts the cover photo to the wearer's outfit, from the original when there is one, at most 900 pixels tall. The operator found the first cuts zoomed out (a stray box, such as a bag on a chair, widened them), so since 2026-11-07 the first reader is asked once per cover photo for a tight box around the wearer, head to lowest clothing or shoe, given the wearer's face box (`outfit_cover_wearer_boxes`; about $0.037 a photo), and the cut is that box with 3% of its height around it, in its own shape between 1:2.4 and 3:4 (`cover_aspect`). Until a photo's box is read the cut is the face and piece boxes with 8% around them (`cover_reason.frame_from: piece_boxes`); `PUT /outfits/{id}/cover` reads a new cover at once, `GET /outfits` starts reading missing ones in the background when the review worker is on, and `POST /outfit-covers/read-wearer-boxes` reads them all. Cutting an original takes about a second, so cuts are kept in `<data dir>/outfit-covers/` (safe to delete), `GET /outfits` cuts the listed ones not cut yet in the background, and two are cut at a time. The frame needs photo-store, so a photo-store outage makes the outfit reads fail rather than answer without a frame. CONTRACT.md "Outfit covers" has the shapes. The owner marks outfits with `favorite` and `tags` (`PATCH /outfits/{id}`, `GET /outfits?favorite=true&tag=`, `GET /outfit-tags`), asked for the same day; a combine carries both to the kept outfit and its undo takes back what it carried. A rebuild from looks makes new outfit ids, which start with neither, as they start with no cover pick.

## Products and eBay orders

**Products** (`products.go`, `product_…`), asked for on 2026-11-07: a make and model as its maker's own listing describes it, which any number of possessions point at (`product_id`); a possession read carries its product's listing (`product`). The page is article-store's: `POST /products` saves `listing_url` there (or takes an `article_id`) and the first reader copies description, specs and list price from the article's text plus the whole fetched page, since readability drops spec tables (about $0.05 a product). Finding the page is a web search an agent does first; prefer the maker's page (`listing_source: manufacturer`) and say how it was found in `found_by`. The first, `product_000001` (Seiko SMW006A), is on Seiko Instruments' music.sii.co.jp, not seiko-watch: Seiko Instruments makes the metronome watches.

**eBay orders are budget-store's, not purchases here.** The operator chose on 2026-11-07 that the purchase is the budget-store transaction that paid for it, and its chip shows the order. `POST /marketplace-orders/import-ebay-mail` reads eBay's order confirmations from mailstack (`MAILSTACK_TOKEN` in the host-local drop-in `30-mailstack-token.conf`; read only) with the first reader (about $0.022 a mail) and posts each order to budget-store's `POST /marketplace-orders`, which finds the charge by the order number in the bank description, else by amount and date. `POST /marketplace-order-lines/match` then has both readers match each open order line to a possession, with pictures: both sure of the same one links it (the possession takes `budget_transaction_id`, and the order's date and the line's price where it has none; budget-store links the line to it), any other pick asks an optional question (`asked_by: purchase_match`). The first run's eBay purchases here (`possession_purchase_000001`–`000101`) were soft-deleted with `DELETE /purchases/{id}`, which withdrew their questions. Purchases stay for other sources. The import runs in this service only because the readers are here; the records are budget-store's.

## Bringing photos in

`possession-add-photos -queued-by principal_… [-time-zone America/Los_Angeles] photo…` copies each original to `<data dir>/originals/<sha256>.<ext>`, adds it to photo-store upright with its EXIF date and GPS, and queues it. It reads JPEG, PNG and WebP, not HEIC. `source_machine` is harness-store's one machine with transport `local`. The readers see the original scaled to 2048 pixels when it is in `originals/`, and photo-store's 1024-pixel thumbnail otherwise. Boxes are read on thumbnails, so an original is turned to stand as its thumbnail does (`alignOriginal` in `images.go`: EXIF orientation, checked against the thumbnail in every orientation, refused when none matches); on 2026-11-07 all 122 originals of the outfit selfies matched by EXIF. Crops for identification come from the full-size picture, and small pieces (jewelry, watches, eyewear) are cut with room and scaled up (`detailCrop`).

# Working in this repo

## Build, test and deploy

`go test ./...` runs everything, with scripted readers in `store_test.go`; `server_test.go` builds the whole mux, so a route collision fails a test rather than the deploy. `./deploy.sh` (through deploy-gate) tests, builds `possession-store` and `possession-add-photos` into `~/bin`, installs the user unit and checks `/health` and the loopback bind. The service refuses to start with the review worker on and no `LLMBRIDGE_SERVICE_TOKEN`.

## Nothing personal in the repo

The repo is on GitHub and may become public. Tests use made-up ids, places and coordinates. What the operator owns lives only in the database.
