# About possession-store

## What it owns

An inventory of the things the operator owns, on `127.0.0.1:8341` with no auth: dash is the front door. `CONTRACT.md` is the route table and the source of truth for every shape.

It owns possessions and their kinds (twenty: garments, computers, computer parts, audio devices, musical instruments, vehicles, furniture, fitness equipment, sports gear, tabletop games, media, collectibles, art and decor, home textiles, appliances, personal care, bags and cases, electronic accessories, household tools, music accessories — a table each, with its own id prefix; `KindTypes` in `records.go` is the list), outfits, purchases and their lines, and the photo review (reviews, model readings, sightings, owner questions). It does not own photos (photo-store), places (location-store), machines (harness-store) or what kind of instrument something is (music-practice-store): it stores their ids and checks each one against the owning store before writing it. Net worth is not here yet; it waits on budget-store.

A new kind is a new table and a new entry in `KindTypes` (`records.go`), never a column holding the kind's name. Add one when enough possessions share facts worth searching on; until then a possession with no kind is complete. The fourteen after vehicles came on 2026-11-06, when the first 52 photos left 223 of 288 possessions with no kind; `POST /possessions/classify-kindless` sorted the ones already recorded. A room (`Rooms`) is on the possession itself, never on a kind.

# How it works

## Photo review

Two model-store roles read each queued photo: `possession-photo-reader` (claude-opus-5-5) and `possession-photo-second-reader` (gpt-6-astra), chosen by the operator on 2026-11-06 so that two makers' models must agree. Each holds one model and no fallback. Calls go through llm-bridge-server's `POST /oneshot` with the service token from the unit's host-local drop-in; costs come from model-store list prices.

Round one: each reader lists what it sees on its own. Round two: each sees both lists and the inventory and writes a final list. `recordReconciliation` (`review.go`) pairs the two lists. What both agree on is recorded at once; a doubt either raised about it becomes an optional **detail** question, whose answer in words amends the possession. The rest waits on a **decision** question whose choices carry their resolution. An answer in words goes to the first reader, which turns it into a resolution. The operator chose on 2026-11-06 that agreement records without waiting: holding agreed things for a detail made 62% of sightings into questions. Settling a sighting settles every later one waiting on it (`same_as_other_sighting`).

The vocabularies in `vocabulary.go` feed both validation and the readers' prompt (`kindGuide`): change them there and nowhere else. A reader's field that does not fit is dropped and named in `dropped_fields`, never stored.

**Outfit reading** (`reading: outfit`, `outfit_reading.go`): one reader, the first role, reads a photo of the wearer (`wearer_person_id`, located by photo-store's confirmed face) against every wearable already recorded. The operator chose one reader for outfits on 2026-11-06; it costs about $0.09 a selfie. New pieces start `unconfirmed` with a "do you still have it?" detail question, because a photo shows the wearer had a thing then, not now. Since 2026-11-07 each piece the reader lists is then matched by picture: both roles see the piece cut from the photo beside the covers of up to 12 recorded wearables of its kind and sub-type (`matchPieces`); both pick the same one, it is linked; both say none, it is new; they differ, a decision question (`asked_by: picture_match`). That adds roughly $0.10 a piece compared (an estimate from token counts, not measured). A reading's pieces find the live outfit with exactly the same pieces, else the same day's outfit sharing the most pieces (at least one), else make a new one, and add a wearing on the photo's day in `POSSESSION_STORE_OWNER_TIME_ZONE`. Wearable kinds (`WearableKinds`): garment, jewelry, watch, eyewear, bag_or_case; an outfit holds nothing else.

## Wearable audit

`wearable_audit.go` and `outfit_consolidation.go` hold three passes over what outfit readings recorded, asked for on 2026-11-07 after one reader matching by text left 65 outfits for 133 selfies, several per day, and likely duplicate pieces: `POST /wearables/check-links`, `POST /wearables/check-duplicates` and `POST /outfits/consolidate`. Each shows pictures to both reader roles. The operator chose: where both agree, act at once, and every merge must be undoable; where they differ, ask a decision question. So a merge of possessions writes a `possession_merge` and a combine of outfits an `outfit_merge`, each naming every row it moved (`merge.go`, `outfit_merge.go`), and `POST /possession-merges/{id}/undo` and `POST /outfit-merges/{id}/undo` put them back, newest first. A question the audit asks carries `asked_by`, and its choices carry resolutions of their own (`auditActions`: `keep_link`, `merge_possessions`, `keep_apart`, `combine_outfits`, `keep_outfits_apart`, and `unlink_possession_id` on accept, same_as or reject); `TriageOutfitDoubts` leaves those alone. A pair or link asked about once is not asked again. CONTRACT.md has the rules for agreement and the batching of large groups.

Agents run the passes on request; nothing schedules them. They answer what they did and `cost_usd`, and run until done, so call them with a long timeout.

**Closed groups and the rematch** (`wearable_groups.go`, `picture_match.go`), asked for on 2026-11-07 after the passes: selfies had left 265 outfit pieces `proposed` behind open decision questions, so they never joined their outfits, and wrist crops matched against close-up covers had made 8 `unconfirmed` watches for an owner of 5. The owner can close a group (`PUT /wearable-groups/{group}`, e.g. `watch`, `jewelry:ring`): then nothing in it is recorded as new anywhere — the picture match shows both readers every owned member with up to 6 photos each and they pick one or say `cannot_tell`, accepting a new piece is a 409, and the link and duplicate checks offer only members. `POST /outfit-pieces/rematch` re-runs the two-reader match on every stuck outfit piece, settling and answering what both agree on (`answered_by: picture_match`), and folds each unconfirmed member of a closed group into the owned member both readers pick, asking otherwise (`asked_by: closed_group`).

## Looks, slots and identification

`looks.go`, `identify.go`, `slot_links.go` and `rebuild.go` replaced the rematch on 2026-11-07, after the operator asked for every outfit to be filled with a best guess before they validate anything. How many pieces the owner has in each group is data: `expected_low`/`expected_high` on `PUT /wearable-groups/{group}`, never in a prompt. Groups follow how the owner counts (`wearableGroup`): pendants and chains are necklaces, scarves are their own group, socks are in none.

Run in order: set the groups' ranges; `POST /outfit-looks/read` (both readers read each day's selfies together into looks, one per set of clothes, and slots, one per piece worn, with a box per photo; the first lists, the second checks and writes the final list); `POST /outfit-slots/identify` (per group, both readers sort every slot crop and recorded cover into individual pieces; the first reader's sorting is the best guess, the second's and both readers' hints rank the rest; each slot is linked to its top candidate with `decided_by: best_guess` and gets one optional question, `asked_by: outfit_slots`, whose answer relinks it); `POST /outfits/rebuild-from-looks` (one outfit per distinct set of linked pieces, the old outfits replaced, undoable through `POST /outfit-rebuilds/{id}/undo`). CONTRACT.md has the scoring, the batch sizes and the rules for merging recorded pieces.

## Outfit covers

`outfit_covers.go`, asked for on 2026-11-07 so dash's outfits page can show each outfit as one tall photo. Every outfit with a photo answers `cover_photo_id`, `cover_box` and `cover_reason`, worked out on each read from boxes already stored, with no model call: of its photos, the one where the most of its pieces have a box in `possession_photos` (slot links and readings both write there), then the one those boxes span most of, then the earliest. The owner's pick (`PUT /outfits/{id}/cover`, `outfit_cover_choices`) wins while that photo is still one of the outfit's; a rebuild or combine makes new outfit ids, which start without a pick. `GET /outfits/{id}/image` cuts the cover photo to the wearer — photo-store's confirmed face of the review's `wearer_person_id` and every piece box, 8% margin, made 2:3 without leaving the photo — from the original when there is one, at most 900 pixels tall. Cutting an original takes about a second, so cuts are kept in `<data dir>/outfit-covers/` (safe to delete), `GET /outfits` cuts the listed ones not cut yet in the background, and two are cut at a time. The frame needs photo-store, so a photo-store outage makes the outfit reads fail rather than answer without a frame. CONTRACT.md "Outfit covers" has the shapes.

## Bringing photos in

`possession-add-photos -queued-by principal_… [-time-zone America/Los_Angeles] photo…` copies each original to `<data dir>/originals/<sha256>.<ext>`, adds it to photo-store upright with its EXIF date and GPS, and queues it. It reads JPEG, PNG and WebP, not HEIC. `source_machine` is harness-store's one machine with transport `local`. The readers see the original scaled to 2048 pixels when it is in `originals/`, and photo-store's 1024-pixel thumbnail otherwise. Boxes are read on thumbnails, so an original is turned to stand as its thumbnail does (`alignOriginal` in `images.go`: EXIF orientation, checked against the thumbnail in every orientation, refused when none matches); on 2026-11-07 all 122 originals of the outfit selfies matched by EXIF. Crops for identification come from the full-size picture, and small pieces (jewelry, watches, eyewear) are cut with room and scaled up (`detailCrop`).

# Working in this repo

## Build, test and deploy

`go test ./...` runs everything, with scripted readers in `store_test.go`; `server_test.go` builds the whole mux, so a route collision fails a test rather than the deploy. `./deploy.sh` (through deploy-gate) tests, builds `possession-store` and `possession-add-photos` into `~/bin`, installs the user unit and checks `/health` and the loopback bind. The service refuses to start with the review worker on and no `LLMBRIDGE_SERVICE_TOKEN`.

## Nothing personal in the repo

The repo is on GitHub and may become public. Tests use made-up ids, places and coordinates. What the operator owns lives only in the database.
